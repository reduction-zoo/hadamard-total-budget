## Correctness

**Source and construction.** In proof.md:8–33 and algorithm.py's subset_product/construct, prime exponents enforce exactly one assignment item per variable, and clause slack exponents 0 through 3 can reach four precisely when at least one literal occurrence is true. Repetitions and opposite signs within a clause are handled by counting true occurrences. Padding to K at least three prevents coincident cycle blocks and supplies the minimum clique sizes. The empty conjunction gives three unit items and target one, hence a legal YES target. The source legality check rejects unused or nonconsecutive declarations; this is a legitimate canonical encoding of 3-SAT. Every valid source produces one nonempty integer matrix and one integer budget, without any oracle call.

The row count is correct. Top anchor rows contribute K(K-1) pair rows; complete rows contribute (s-2)s(s-1)/2; columns contribute s(s-1)(s-2)/2; local, calibration and cycle rows contribute 6K. Adding the identity yields the stated mu. The code generates these exact families, with no accidental overlap between consecutive cycle blocks.

**All rank allocations.** Proof.md:37–47 is sufficient for arbitrary real factors. The leading identity forces rank(M)=s². For any widths p,q with p+q at most 2s, the chain s² <= rank(A)rank(B) <= pq <= (p+q)²/4 <= s² makes every inequality an equality. Thus p=q=s and both actual ranks are s. Zero widths, unequal widths, spare widths and lower budgets cannot escape. This applies also to witnesses initially specified as full matrices, since their ranks provide such widths. Identity row tensors and column tensors are dual bases in the now exact s²-dimensional space. Therefore later row coefficients determine their full tensors, not merely projections.

**Anchor orientation.** Proof.md:51–82 does not assume the grid it needs to prove. The rank-one sum lemma applies to every pair of independent identity tensors. Three pairwise adjacent independent tensors have a unique common factor side. A complete row and a column intersect once; equal sides would put 2s-2 independent tensors in a space of dimension s. Since every complete row intersects every column, all complete rows orient alike and all columns oppositely. Each top row meets applicable columns, and its union with any such column contains K+s-2>s independent tensors. This forces its side as well. These strict inequalities hold at K=3.

Once oriented, a complete row proves that all s right lines are independent. If the top two left lines coincided, every local neighbor would share that left line: otherwise it would have to share both distinct right lines of its anchors. The entire identity basis would then lie in a tensor space of dimension at most s(s-1), a contradiction. With distinct top left lines, every neighbor occupies one of two off-diagonal corners; the two neighbors cannot occupy the same corner because they are independent. Finally all identity tensors use at most s left lines, and their full span forces these lines to be independent. This establishes the global right-block independence required for cycle composition, even though many top grid cells were left unanchored.

**Selectors, composition and completeness.** Proof.md:86–113 has the necessary nonzero premises. After absorbing anchor scales, calibration gives reciprocal multipliers in both orientations. The three local coefficient patterns have zero determinant, with well-defined left-line ratios h_i, r_i^epsilon_i h_i, and T h_i. All item values and T are positive nonzero integers, but h_i and the factors need not be positive. Independence of the right blocks makes a two-block cycle row rank one exactly when these ratios agree. Multiplication around the cycle cancels nonzero h_i and gives the required subset equation. Consequently a NO formula has no feasible real decomposition at any allowed allocation.

For a YES formula, the prescribed identity tensors are a scaled permutation of a coordinate tensor basis. Their duals are consequently product vectors; this does not rely on an unproved claim that arbitrary product bases have product duals. Anchor sums, local pair sums, calibrations and cycles all have rank-one reshapes. Factoring these rows and using the explicit duals reproduces every matrix entry, including zeros. This proves the other Karp direction.

**Universal encoded recovery.** Proof.md:117–133 and extract agree. Identity rows 0 and 2 are L_0,L_1; in the oriented side they are proportional, while their other sides are independent. The implemented test therefore selects the correct factor space even after exchanging entire factors. Rows 2i+1 are exactly the X_i selectors. Comparing each with L_0 detects the chosen corner, independent of coordinate bases or nonzero row scales. The assignment prime forces exactly one selected item in each variable pair; clause primes imply satisfaction. The explicit output assertions are therefore guaranteed for every valid witness, rather than substitutes for the converse proof.

The gcd/root-count zero test localizes to the supplied root of a possibly reducible, repeated defining polynomial. It works when the coordinate determinant is identically zero, has greater degree than the defining polynomial, or vanishes at only the isolated root's irreducible factor. Endpoint non-root premises pass to every gcd divisor. Square-free reduction removes multiplicities, and zero endpoint sequence evaluations are omitted in sign variation. Literal dense encodings prevent compressed degrees or coefficient magnitudes. The current rule adds vector line tests to the unchanged arithmetic, without constructing new algebraic extensions.

**Runtime and bits.** The dense matrix has O(S^5) entries for S=n+m+2; entries have O(S log S) bits. Prime generation by the actual trial-division loop and all integer products are polynomial. The stated O((ell+2)^10) forward bound and O((ell+2)^7) output bound are conservative. Full completeness witnesses have polynomial length because prefix products have polynomial bit length.

For an arbitrary encoded witness of length H, each input degree and coefficient height is O(H). Cross-products have degree O(H), and clearing denominators has polynomial height. The inherited subresultant argument bounds subsequent coefficients by Sylvester determinant bounds, normalizes before root counting, and uses polynomial-size square-free division and sign-corrected subresultants. Installed euclid_amv applies Collins–Brown–Traub coefficient reduction; sturm_amv(method=1) changes the necessary signs. Thus the actual called procedure, not a hypothetical alternate decoder, has polynomial bit cost. The inherited exponent 24 is a loose adequate bound; multiplying by O((n+1)s) zero tests gives the stated backward bound. Re-parsing coordinates and reading the full witness are covered. No polynomial source-length bound on arbitrary supplied witness size is needed.

## Significance

Judgment: **pass**. Unlike the fixed-width theorem, this reduction proves NP-hardness for the target's actual minimum rank sum, and its NO direction excludes every allocation under the budget. It does so for nonnegative integer matrices, exact equality, two independent unrestricted-sign real factors, and polynomial witness recovery under the fixed encoding. The anchor-frame lemma provides a concrete mathematical mechanism beyond changing a parameter in the prior result.

The theorem does not establish strong NP-hardness, approximation hardness, hardness with a variable number of factors, both ranks constant, or NP membership for all unrestricted target inputs. The target dimensions and budget grow polynomially. Those limits are compatible with the fixed significance criteria. The unchanged candidate may advance to writing and expert-review preparation.
